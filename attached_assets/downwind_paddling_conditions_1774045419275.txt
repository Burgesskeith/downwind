IDEAL CONDITIONS FOR DOWNWIND PADDLING

Wind: - Ideal speed: 15–25 knots - 10–15 knots: smaller, slower runs
(beginner-friendly) - 20–30 knots: fast, powerful runs (advanced) -
Consistent wind is best - Direction should align with your paddling
route (tailwind or slight angle)

Swell & Bumps: - Wind swell is key (short-period waves) - Ideal period:
6–10 seconds - Ideal height: 1–3 meters - Best when wind swell aligns
with ground swell - Look for “linked runners” to maintain glide

Direction Alignment: - Best: wind and swell moving in same direction -
Acceptable: slight angle - Avoid: - Cross swell (messy) - Opposing swell
(kills glide)

Water State: - Look for organized swell lines and defined bumps -
Whitecaps indicate usable wind energy - Avoid flat water or chaotic chop

Fetch: - Longer fetch = bigger, more organized runners - Open ocean
conditions generally provide better runs

Safety: - Avoid offshore winds (can push you out to sea) - Prefer
side-onshore or manageable conditions - Be aware of exit points and
surroundings

Skill Levels: - Beginner: 10–15 knots, small swell - Intermediate: 15–20
knots, organized runners - Advanced: 20–30+ knots, open ocean

Perfect Conditions Summary: - 18–25 knots steady wind - Long fetch -
Aligned wind and swell - 1–2.5 m swell - Clean, linkable runners - Safe
coastline with exit points
